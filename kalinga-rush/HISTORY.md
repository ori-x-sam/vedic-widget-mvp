# HISTORY.md — sources and verification notes

Every lesson card in `src/data/records.ts` points here with a source key (`sources: [...]`).
Card text must match what is written here. Rules (brief §3, §7):

- **HISTORY** cards are dated and sourced; `c.` marks approximate dates; never a more precise date than the sources give.
- **TRADITION / LEGEND** cards are labelled as such and never presented as documented history.
- **SPECULATIVE** cards (Act VII) are labelled `SPECULATIVE · NOT HISTORY`.
- No new dated fact goes into the game unless it is verified and cited here.

## golbai-sasan — Record 01 · c. 1500 BCE · "Boat-builders on the Chilika shore."

**Card text (as shipped):** At Golbai Sasan, on the Malaguni river just north of Chilika lagoon, archaeologists dug up
many woodworking adzes — tools for shaping logs. So many adzes suggest this was a boat-building centre. The people
here lived in round wooden huts with floors of rammed earth.

**Exercise:** Many woodworking ____ found at Golbai Sasan suggest it was a boat-building centre. → *adzes*.

**Checked (16 Oct 2026):**
- Location: Golabai / Golbai Sasan sits on the north bank of the Malaguni river, north of Chilika — Wikipedia, *Golabai*; *Chilika Lake*.
- Woodworking adzes → interpreted as a boat-building centre; Chilika Lake article notes Golabai is the excavated
  site in Odisha where boat building is documented — Wikipedia, *Golabai*; *Chilika Lake*; *Boita*.
- Round wooden huts with rammed-earth floors — Wikipedia, *Golabai* (citing a 2018 archaeobotany study).
- 2011 excavation exposed ~8 m of Neolithic deposits — UCL "Rice" project, *Golbai Sassan excavations 2011*
  (ucl.ac.uk/rice/fieldwork/golbai-sassan-excavations-2011).

**Date note:** sources disagree. One gives the earliest level at ~2300 BCE by radiocarbon, another puts the
Neolithic around 1600 BCE, another at ~3500 BP. The brief's display date **c. 1500 BCE** stands for "the
2nd millennium BCE"; the card never claims more precision than that. The card text deliberately says
"woodworking adzes" (as the sources do); the scene shows polished stone adzes as the brief describes.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golabai · https://www.ucl.ac.uk/rice/fieldwork/golbai-sassan-excavations-2011 ·
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boita

## chilika — Record 01 "Did you know?"

**Card text:** Chilika is often called Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon: river water and sea water mix there, and
Irrawaddy dolphins swim in it.

**Checked (16 Oct 2026):** commonly described as Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon (hence "often called");
India's first Ramsar site (1981); the Irrawaddy dolphin is the lagoon's flagship species; Nalabana island is the
core bird sanctuary where flamingos and other migratory birds winter. Figures for area and rank vary between
sources, so the card avoids numbers.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilika_Lake · https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/important-facts-for-prelims-27th-october-2018 ·
https://livinglakes.org/lake-chilika/

Scene notes: flamingos, egrets, Irrawaddy dolphins (blunt rounded head, no beak, small dorsal fin) and lotus
are shown as Chilika wildlife; no temples appear in this record (none existed at c. 1500 BCE).
