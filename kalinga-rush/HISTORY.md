# HISTORY.md — sources and verification notes

Every lesson card in `src/data/records.ts` points here with a source key (`sources: [...]`).
Card text must match what is written here. Rules (brief §3, §7):

- **HISTORY** cards are dated and sourced; `c.` marks approximate dates; never a more precise date than the sources give.
- **TRADITION / LEGEND** cards are labelled as such and never presented as documented history.
- **SPECULATIVE** cards (Act VII) are labelled `SPECULATIVE · NOT HISTORY`.
- No new dated fact goes into the game unless it is verified and cited here.

## golbai-sasan — Record 01 · c. 1500 BCE · "Boat-builders on the Chilika shore."

**Card text (as shipped):** At Golbai Sasan, on the Malaguni river just north of Chilika lagoon, archaeologists dug up
many woodworking adzes — tools for shaping logs. So many adzes suggest this was a boat-building centre. The people
here lived in round wooden huts with floors of rammed earth.

**Exercise:** Many woodworking ____ found at Golbai Sasan suggest it was a boat-building centre. → *adzes*.

**Checked (16 Oct 2026):**
- Location: Golabai / Golbai Sasan sits on the north bank of the Malaguni river, north of Chilika — Wikipedia, *Golabai*; *Chilika Lake*.
- Woodworking adzes → interpreted as a boat-building centre; Chilika Lake article notes Golabai is the excavated
  site in Odisha where boat building is documented — Wikipedia, *Golabai*; *Chilika Lake*; *Boita*.
- Round wooden huts with rammed-earth floors — Wikipedia, *Golabai* (citing a 2018 archaeobotany study).
- 2011 excavation exposed ~8 m of Neolithic deposits — UCL "Rice" project, *Golbai Sassan excavations 2011*
  (ucl.ac.uk/rice/fieldwork/golbai-sassan-excavations-2011).

**Date note:** sources disagree. One gives the earliest level at ~2300 BCE by radiocarbon, another puts the
Neolithic around 1600 BCE, another at ~3500 BP. The brief's display date **c. 1500 BCE** stands for "the
2nd millennium BCE"; the card never claims more precision than that. The card text deliberately says
"woodworking adzes" (as the sources do); the scene shows polished stone adzes as the brief describes.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golabai · https://www.ucl.ac.uk/rice/fieldwork/golbai-sassan-excavations-2011 ·
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boita

## chilika — Record 01 "Did you know?"

**Card text:** Chilika is often called Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon: river water and sea water mix there, and
Irrawaddy dolphins swim in it.

**Checked (16 Oct 2026):** commonly described as Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon (hence "often called");
India's first Ramsar site (1981); the Irrawaddy dolphin is the lagoon's flagship species; Nalabana island is the
core bird sanctuary where flamingos and other migratory birds winter. Figures for area and rank vary between
sources, so the card avoids numbers.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilika_Lake · https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/important-facts-for-prelims-27th-october-2018 ·
https://livinglakes.org/lake-chilika/

Scene notes: flamingos, egrets, Irrawaddy dolphins (blunt rounded head, no beak, small dorsal fin) and lotus
are shown as Chilika wildlife; no temples appear in this record (none existed at c. 1500 BCE).

**Did you know?** Chilika is often called Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon: river water and sea water mix there, and Irrawaddy dolphins swim in it.

**Exercise:** Many woodworking ____ found at Golbai Sasan suggest it was a boat-building centre. → *adzes*

**Date note:** Estimates span the 2nd millennium BCE; displayed as "c. 1500 BCE".

**Sources:** `golbai-sasan` (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golabai · ucl.ac.uk/rice/fieldwork/golbai-sassan-excavations-2011); `chilika` (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilika_Lake · livinglakes.org/lake-chilika)

### r02 — Lesson 2 · c. 300 BCE · "A city of eight gates."

*HISTORY · Act 1 · Sisupalgarh, near Bhubaneswar · ride: cart*

**Card:** Sisupalgarh, near today's Bhubaneswar, was a nearly square fortified city of about 1.2 square kilometres. Its walls rose a little over 9 metres, with a moat outside and eight brick-and-stone gateways — two on each side. It is identified with Tosali, also called Kalinganagari.

**Did you know?** Sisupalgarh was first excavated by B.B. Lal in 1948, and estimates put its population at 20,000–25,000 people.

**Exercise:** Sisupalgarh had eight gateways, two on each side. → *True*

**Checked:** Near-square fortified city ~1.2 km², walls a little over 9 m, eight gateways (two per side), moat; identified with Tosali/Kalinganagari; excavated by B.B. Lal in 1948; population estimate 20,000–25,000.

**Sources:** `sisupalgarh` (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisupalgarh · livehistoryindia.com/story/places/sisupalgarh-the-lost-city)

### r03 — Lesson 3 · Kartika Purnima · "The boitas sail for the golden islands."

*HISTORY · Act 1 · An estuary port on the Kalinga coast · ride: boita*

**Card:** Kalinga's merchant-mariners, the sadhabas, sailed to Sri Lanka, Java, Sumatra, Borneo, Bali and Burma with goods such as pepper, cinnamon, cardamom, silk, camphor and gold. The Bay of Bengal was once called the Sea of Kalinga. They set out on Kartika Purnima, when the winds favoured the voyage.

**Did you know?** The chant "ā kā mā bai" names the four sacred months — Asadha, Kartika, Magha and Baisakha. The voyages are remembered today in Boita Bandana and Cuttack's Bali Jatra.

**Exercise:** The Bay of Bengal was once called the Sea of ____. → *Kalinga*

**Sources:** `maritime-odisha` (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_history_of_Odisha · en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boita); `boita-bandana` (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boita_Bandana · en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali_Jatra)

### r04 — Lesson 4 · 261 BCE · "The Kalinga War. An emperor lays down the sword."

*HISTORY · Act 1 · Dhauli hill above the Daya river · ride: foot*

**Card:** Emperor Ashoka's Rock Edict XIII records the terrible loss of life in his war against Kalinga. Afterwards he turned away from war towards dhamma, which he spread through edicts carved on rocks and pillars.

**Did you know?** Special "Kalinga edicts" are carved at Dhauli and at Jaugada. At Dhauli, an elephant is carved out of the living rock above them.

**Exercise:** Ashoka's Rock Edict ____ records the loss of life in the Kalinga War. → *XIII*

**Checked:** Rock Edict XIII records the losses; the separate "Kalinga edicts" are at Dhauli and Jaugada; the Dhauli elephant is carved from the rock above the edicts. No battle is shown.

**Sources:** `kalinga-war` (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_War · en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhauli)

### r05 — Lesson 5 · c. 2nd–1st century BCE · "Kharavela writes his story in stone."

*HISTORY · Act 1 · Udayagiri and Khandagiri hills · ride: elephant*

**Card:** On Udayagiri hill near Bhubaneswar, the 17-line Hathigumpha ('Elephant Cave') inscription, in Prakrit and Brahmi script, tells the life of King Kharavela of Kalinga. It says he brought back the 'Kalinga Jina' image that a Nanda king had carried away.

**Did you know?** Udayagiri and Khandagiri are twin hills full of rock-cut caves. One of them, the Rani Gumpha, has two storeys.

**Exercise:** The Hathigumpha inscription is written in ____ script. → *Brahmi*

**Checked:** 17-line Prakrit inscription in Brahmi script; mentions the Kalinga Jina brought back from the Nandas. Date given as c. 2nd–1st century BCE because scholars disagree.

**Sources:** `hathigumpha` (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hathigumpha_inscription · en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udayagiri_and_Khandagiri_Caves)

*HISTORY · Act 2 · Bhubaneswar · ride: cart*
